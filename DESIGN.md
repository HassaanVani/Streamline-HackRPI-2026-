# Streamline design context

Read `docs/ux-research.md` and `docs/ui-audit.md` before redesigning the experience.

The audience is a curious visitor, including a hackathon judge, who may know little aviation terminology. This is an assumed audience; peer testing remains necessary. The main task is to explore how winds affect hypothetical cruise routes and understand the result.

- Keep the map as the workspace. No introductory marketing page, feature-card grid, decorative live indicator, or simulated operational status.
- Default to pale sky water, cloud-white geography and panels, navy text and teal alternatives. Keep ATC as an optional palette. Preserve persistent text labels and solid versus dashed paths.
- Give the eye a task: choose a journey in Free explore or predict and test in Wind challenges. Do not show every tool simultaneously.
- Use city names with codes. Explain along-route wind, ground speed and counterfactuals in context. Avoid theatrical aviation jargon.
- Reward experiments completed, not clicks, duration on page or fictitious real-world performance. Derive outcomes from actual scenario metrics. Replays do not farm first-attempt credit.
- Every state needs an honest identity: pending, failed, saved, computed, wind off, or changed selection. A saved example is never the user's failed requested route.
- New primary actions and answer targets should be at least 44px high. Keep keyboard equivalents, visible focus and reduced-motion behavior.
- Stay explicit about frozen GFS winds, 250 hPa, 230m/s illustrative airspeed, and operational feasibility not assessed. Do not present the solver as generative AI or live aircraft tracking.
- Do not claim user validation until people have actually tested it. Preserve the test tasks and record failures.
